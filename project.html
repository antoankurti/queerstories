On the work

Each city makes its own promises to its queers. Some deliver. Most do not, or not entirely, or not for everyone.

Beneath official architecture and public space, every city holds another geography, less visible, less named, built from encounters and routes that leave no trace on any map. It is there, too, that queer life takes shape.

I knew you existed documents both portraits simultaneously: the city as physical and political fact, and the people who inhabit it. A cartography not of monuments, but of margins: voices, bodies, faces, accumulated through video, photography, audio and text, without hierarchy, without editorial conclusion.

Yet the project is also a polyphony. Across cities and continents, individual experiences echo one another: the same fears spoken in different languages, the same desires described from different streets, the same turning points lived in different decades. From these overlapping fragments, a collective story becomes audible, one that does not erase the local but makes the universal visible.

I knew you existed is an open, itinerant work. It began in São Paulo in 2020. It will continue wherever there is a community willing to speak, and a city worth reading.

How

Each testimony begins with a conversation, most often passed from one person to the next: someone who participated, recommending the project to someone they trusted. Word of mouth is the primary method. Dating apps and queer associations have also opened doors, and some conversations continued at home, when an invitation was extended. These were often the most candid.

The format is chosen by the person speaking: video, recorded voice, photograph, written text sent days later. There is no hierarchy among these. Each carries what another cannot. A voice recording preserves what a transcript would smooth over: hesitation, silence, laughter in the wrong place. Text, written in the days following a meeting, sometimes captures what the moment itself did not allow.

The places, too, are chosen by those who know them. A cruising spot, a bar that no longer exists, a park bench, a neighbourhood that changed. Participants show the city as they inhabit it, not as it presents itself. The architectural documentation that runs alongside follows the same logic: the city as a lived and remembered space, not as a catalogue of landmarks. The two readings are meant to coexist.

Collaborate

I knew you existed exists to connect, across cities, continents, and decades, experiences that placed alongside one another begin to sound like a single story. Each new city adds a layer to that story, and a city already on the map can always receive another.

What makes this possible is both access and material support: access to people willing to speak and spaces willing to open, and residencies, travel grants, or co-productions that make it possible to go where the project needs to go.

If you have a community willing to speak and a city worth reading, or simply a group of friends who want to put their lives on the map, the project is listening.

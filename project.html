Each city makes its own promises to its queers. Some deliver. Most do not, or not entirely, or not for everyone.

Beneath official architecture and public space, every city holds another geography, less visible, less named, built from encounters and routes that leave no trace on any map. It is there, too, that queer life takes shape.

I knew you existed documents both portraits simultaneously: the city as physical and political fact, and the people who inhabit it. A cartography not of monuments, but of margins: voices, bodies, faces, accumulated through video, photography, audio and text, without hierarchy, without editorial conclusion.

Yet the project is also a polyphony. Across cities and continents, individual experiences echo one another: the same fears spoken in different languages, the same desires described from different streets, the same turning points lived in different decades. From these overlapping fragments, a collective story becomes audible, one that does not erase the local but makes the universal visible.

I knew you existed is an open, itinerant work. It began in São Paulo in 2020. It will continue wherever there is a community willing to speak, and a city worth reading.
